## Derived RGB benchmark input

Only the RGB stream is used as the input demonstration:

```text
data/raw/sources/EM1-0294.r3d   # original source archive; Git-ignored
data/raw/place_demo.mp4         # RGB-only derivative; Git-ignored
data/interim/place_demo/        # 10 FPS frames + manifest; Git-ignored
```

The MP4 contains 481 frames at 1920×1440, 30 FPS, and 16.03 seconds. It was sampled
to 161 frames at 10 FPS for Phase 1. Depth, confidence, and camera-pose channels are
not consumed by the primary RGB pipeline; they may be held out for later validation
only, consistent with the project's monocular-input claim.

## Why it fits

- One stable, near-egocentric iPhone RGB viewpoint.
- One green source container and one visually distinct white bowl target.
- Clear empty-hand initial state, grasp, transfer, placement inside the bowl, release,
  and stable final state.
- The full sequence falls within the 10–20 second benchmark contract.
- The original capture provides a high-quality reference for debugging geometry later.

Approximate timing: the hand approaches and grasps at 3–5 s, transfers the container
through the middle of the clip, and releases it in the bowl by roughly 11 s.

## Limitations and controls

- The container is close to rotationally symmetric around its vertical axis. It is
  acceptable for Place and translation tracking, but it is a weak choice for a strict
  yaw-accuracy or track-loss rotation ablation.
- The glass tabletop is reflective. Log reflection-induced mask or depth failures in
  [`failure_analysis.md`](../failure_analysis.md) rather than silently discarding them.
- The bowl is a stronger success target than a flat taped region, but it adds partial
  occlusion near placement. Evaluate source-mask quality both before and after release.
- Do not commit the source archive, derived MP4, frames, or masks to this repository.
  Publish only code, configuration, non-derivative plots, and a source link unless the
  dataset license explicitly permits the intended media use.

## Follow-up decision

Proceed with segmentation and point tracking on this episode. Before claiming a full
6D tracking result, add a second test object with asymmetric texture or geometry for
the rotation-sensitive ablation.
